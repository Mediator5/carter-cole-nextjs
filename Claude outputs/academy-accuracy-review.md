## 2 · Must change — the deck is wrong for our season

### The due-diligence penalty figure

The deck teaches **$650 per failure, $2,600 maximum**. That is the amount for
returns **filed in 2026**. Our preparers file in **2027**.

The penalty is adjusted for inflation every year and the 2027 figure is not
published yet. Teaching a fixed number guarantees the class learns a number
that will be out of date before they use it.

**Fix applied:** the slides now teach the *structure* — one penalty per credit,
four credits possible on a single return, the firm is assessable too — and
send preparers to look up the current amount rather than recall it. The exact
figure goes on the Verify Before You File slide as something to check each
October.

## 3 · Gap — missing content that matters here

### Car loan interest deduction

Schedule 1-A has **four** deductions. The deck teaches three and omits the
fourth entirely.

- Up to **$10,000** of interest on a qualifying car loan
- Phases out from **$100,000–$150,000** single, **$200,000–$250,000** MFJ
- **New vehicles only**, assembled in the United States
- Purchased between **1 January 2025 and 31 December 2028**
- Claimed whether the client itemises or takes the standard deduction

This is Detroit. Clients finance American-assembled vehicles. Preparers will
meet this in their first week and would have had no idea it existed.

**Fix applied:** new teaching slide in Module 5 and a scenario.

### American Opportunity Credit

Form 8867 covers four things: EIC, CTC/ACTC/ODC, **AOTC**, and head of
household. The deck covers EITC and filing status thoroughly, the child
credits in passing, and the education credit barely at all — yet it carries the
same per-failure penalty.

**Recommendation:** not fixed in this pass. Either add a short AOTC due
diligence slide to Module 3, or make it an explicit escalation item — "we do
not prepare education credits without review." Lashanda's call, and worth a
decision before October.

## 4 · Verify before 1 October

Things that are correct today but may move, or that I could not confirm for
TY2026. Someone should check these with the IRS site open before the first
class.

1. **Whether the tips, overtime and senior amounts are inflation-adjusted for
   TY2026.** The statute runs 2025–2028. The 2025 figures are confirmed; I
   could not find clear published 2026 amounts. They may be identical. Check
   the Schedule 1-A instructions when released.
2. **The 2027 due-diligence penalty amount** — published on IRS.gov/Form8867.
3. **The PTIN fee for 2027.** Renewal opens around mid-October. The deck says
   $18.75; that has changed before.
4. **Michigan and Detroit figures** — Homestead Property Tax Credit limits, the
   Detroit resident and non-resident rates, and confirmation that Forms 5118 /
   5119 / 5120 are unchanged for TY2026.
5. **Standard mileage rate for TY2026** — used in the Schedule C module.

None of these block the October start. All of them should be confirmed before
the module that teaches them.

## 5 · One thing worth saying out loud

The deck now teaches preparers *not to trust the numbers in it*. That is
deliberate, and it is the single most useful habit in this job. Figures change
every year; the method does not. A preparer who looks it up is worth more than
one who remembers last year's amount confidently.
